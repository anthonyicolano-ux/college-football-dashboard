A practical weekly routine

  1. Open Weekly Rankings, choose the week and Passing or Rushing. Start with Season and the three-game minimum.
  2. Compare Balanced with Efficiency first. Expand Setting sensitivity to see all eight combinations; check how many are eligible and how large each slate is.
  3. Inspect the offense and defense efficiency percentages, yards/game, attempts and opponent samples. A high score can come from volume even if the efficiency pairing is less favorable.
  4. Click Open matchup. Confirm which team is the offense. Inspect passing and rushing cards and the appropriate QB, RB, WR or TE players.
  5. Click a player’s name to inspect recent form and game logs. Check recorded-game coverage and usage distribution before drawing conclusions.

The screens prioritize comparisons. They do not predict yards, touchdowns, player availability or game outcomes.

What makes an efficiency matchup favorable?

For the offense you’re evaluating, above 100% on both sides supports the efficiency pairing.
Offense above 100% means it produced yards more efficiently than opponents usually allowed. Defense above 100% means it allowed opponents to produce yards more efficiently than their usual baseline.

Passing example

Offense 115% · opposing defense 120%

In this hypothetical example, the offense exceeded its efficiency baseline by 15%, while the defense allowed efficiency 20% above its opponents’ baselines. Inspect pass attempts, yards, touchdowns, interceptions and the quarterback’s recorded sample next.

Rushing example

Offense 135% · opposing defense 80%

Here the offense exceeded its baseline, but the defense held opponents below theirs. This is a mixed efficiency pairing. A high balanced score could still reflect rushing volume; check carries, usage split and the efficiency-first view.

Examples are illustrative. Do not multiply the percentages together to project output. Lower defensive efficiency is stronger defense; a higher defensive allowance can be favorable for the opposing offense.

Metric guide

MetricMeaningHow to interpret it
Yards/GObserved yards per selected eligible team game.Combines efficiency and opportunity volume.
Yards/attempt or carryTotal yards divided by total attempts or carries.Raw efficiency; a different opponent mix can affect it.
Yards vs expectedActual yards divided by opponent-specific expected yardage, expressed as a percentage.Above 100% is more offensive production or more defensive allowance than the baseline. Uses matched usable games.
Efficiency vs expectedActual yards versus expected yards at the observed attempt counts, using opponents’ other-game rates.Separates efficiency from attempt volume. Above 100% favors the offense on either side of the comparison.
Attempts/GPassing or rushing opportunities per selected team game.Volume, not total snaps or plays per minute.
Attempts vs baselineObserved attempts versus opponents’ other-game attempt volume.Describes opportunity volume; it is not inherently good or bad.
Rank #1Strongest defense or offense for that page’s ranking metric.Defensive ranks favor lower production allowed. Offensive ranks favor higher production. Check which metric and eligible pool a rank describes.
Min opponent gamesSmallest other-game sample behind the opponent baseline.A team with three games can still have a one-game opponent baseline. This is not a confidence estimate.
Player stat gamesGames with recorded passing, rushing or receiving statistics.Not confirmed appearances. Per-recorded-game averages can differ from averages across all team games.
Carry share / reception sharePlayer counts divided by corresponding team counts in matching recorded games.Describes usage in the available records, not snap share or target share.
Stat coverageHow many selected or recorded games reported a field’s inputs.Zero is a reported value. Missing fields stay unavailable; complete inputs can still have no rate if the denominator is zero.
Score / 100Weighted national percentile comparisons of the offense and opposing defensive allowance.Not a probability, projected yardage total or calibrated confidence score.
Setting sensitivityRanks under two scoring views, two windows and two sample minimums.Inspect rank range, eligible screens and slate sizes. These overlapping comparisons are not historical backtesting.

Samples, missing data and refreshes

  • Statistics use completed FBS-versus-FBS games. Games against FCS opponents are excluded.
  • Last 3 means each team’s own latest three eligible games, not the latest three calendar weeks. Opponent baselines use other completed eligible games available at refresh time.
  • A dash means unavailable, not zero. “No recorded offensive stats” does not establish whether a player appeared or was injured.
  • Targets, injuries and snap counts are not included in the matchup scores. Current roster positions do not establish starters.
  • The refresh banner reports the latest published attempt. A cancelled run or failed status publication may be absent. Source data can lag even after a successful refresh.
  • API counts shown by the dashboard begin when tracking was installed. They exclude earlier runs, other projects and unpublished counts.
  • Refreshing a page, changing filters or sharing the link does not call CFBD. The scheduled or manually started data-update workflow makes API requests.
