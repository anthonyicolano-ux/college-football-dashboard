How to interpret setting sensitivity

Each offensive direction is screened under eight combinations: Balanced/Efficiency first × Season/Last 3 × minimum 3/2 selected games on both teams. Expand its Setting sensitivity column to see every rank, score, eligible slate size, and exclusion reason. The main table still follows your selected settings.

The rank range uses eligible screens only. A low range suggests similar placement under these specific settings; a wide range indicates the ranking depends more on the settings. Top 5 counts refer to these overlapping screens, including tied ranks. They are not independent trials, confidence estimates, odds, or a historical accuracy test. Population size can change with the sample filter, so compare eligible slate sizes along with ranks. A two-game-only pick should not be described as supported across the full set.

This examines settings against the same currently published data and selected week's slate. It does not test past pregame forecasts, injuries, targets, different weight sets beyond the two stated views, or future outcomes. Season and Last 3 overlap and can be identical early in the season. Historical backtesting with data available before kickoff remains an open to-do.

Scoring weights and sample rules

For every eligible offense and defense in the selected statistical window, each metric becomes a national percentile. Higher offensive values and higher defensive values allowed favor the offensive matchup. Lower interception rate favors the offense on either side. Ties use the middle percentile. The offense and opposing defense have equal weight within each group.

Efficiency combines adjusted efficiency and raw yards per attempt/carry at 8:1 weights. Production combines adjusted yardage percentage and raw yards/game at 3:2. Volume equally combines attempts/game and attempts versus baseline. Passing context uses completion percentage, touchdowns/game and inverse interception rate at 1:5:4. Rushing context uses touchdowns/game. These overlapping statistics are deliberate descriptive components, not independent predictive evidence.

Missing optional context fields are omitted and remaining context weights are normalized. Coverage is shown in each score breakdown; absent context on one side uses the available side. Missing core metrics, incomplete adjustment coverage, or insufficient selected games exclude that offensive direction. The national percentile population uses the same eligibility rules. Search keeps the slate's ranks fixed. There is no hidden recent-game blend or sample shrinkage. The alternate-view rank shows sensitivity to the two stated weight sets.

Minimum opponent games uses the efficiency baseline's smallest other-game sample. A three-game team can still have a one-game opponent baseline. These are sample indicators, not calibrated confidence. Last 3 uses each team's own latest eligible games. Baselines use opponents' other completed FBS games through the last refresh, excluding the evaluated game. All results are FBS-versus-FBS only.

Weeks run Monday–Sunday in America/Chicago. Known past kickoffs are hidden. TBD times use their listed date; games with no date are excluded and counted above. The schedule may change between refreshes. Player usage is available through Open matchup; it does not enter these team scores. Targets, injuries, game script and true pace are not included. Scores and rankings are provisional screening heuristics that require historical backtesting before any predictive interpretation.
